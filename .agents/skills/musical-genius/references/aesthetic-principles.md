# Aesthetic principles and evidential status

This note translates findings from music cognition into composition guidance.
The findings constrain plausible design choices; they do not establish a
culture-independent formula for beauty.

## Intermediate predictive complexity

Listeners often prefer musical material that is neither fully predictable nor
unmanageably uncertain. Gold et al. found quadratic relationships between
information-theoretic measures of melodic expectation and liking, with
preferences concentrated in intermediate predictive-complexity regions.

**Compositional implication:** establish learnable regularities, then make
surprises interpretable as revisions of those regularities.

- Benjamin P. Gold et al., “Predictability and Uncertainty in the Pleasure of
  Music: A Reward for Learning?”, *Journal of Neuroscience* 39(47), 2019.
  DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0428-19.2019.

## Groove as expectation plus deviation

Research on groove reports an inverted-U relation between rhythmic complexity
and the pleasurable urge to move. Meter familiarity changes where that optimum
lies: unfamiliar or asymmetric metres often require simpler surface rhythms.

**Compositional implication:** preserve a perceptible beat and grouping
hierarchy; introduce syncopation or displacement against that hierarchy rather
than in place of it.

- Jan Stupacher et al., “The sweet spot between predictability and surprise:
  musical groove in brain, body, and social interactions”, *Frontiers in
  Psychology* 13, 2022. DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2022.906190.
- Daniel J. Levitin, Parag Chordia and Vinod Menon, “Musical rhythm spectra from
  Bach to Joplin obey a 1/f power law”, *PNAS* 109(10), 2012.
  DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1113828109.

## Predictability can be distributed across dimensions

Analyses of larger musical forms suggest that engagement can be sustained when
uncertainty in one domain is counterweighted by stability in another: harmonic
surprise may coexist with motivic repetition, or rhythmic disruption with a
stable harmonic background.

**Compositional implication:** do not maximise novelty simultaneously in
harmony, rhythm, texture, register and form.

- Chen-Gia Tsai, “Predictive Processing within Music Form: Analysis of
  Uncertainty and Surprise in Different Sections of Sonata Form”,
  *Music & Science* 7, 2024. DOI: 10.1177/20592043241267076.

## Repetition supports segmentation and emotional continuity

Experimental comparisons of identical repetition, related variation and
contrast show that listeners track repeated structure and that these relations
affect continuous arousal judgements and phrase-boundary perception.

**Compositional implication:** use recurrence to establish memory and
segmentation, then vary material in proportion to its formal function.

- Steven R. Livingstone, Caroline Palmer and Emery Schubert, “Emotional response
  to musical repetition”, *Emotion* 12(3), 2012.
  DOI: 10.1037/a0023747.

## Tension is multivariate

Perceived tension depends on interactions among register, contour, loudness and
tempo rather than on one parameter in isolation. Low register and increased
loudness can strongly alter how other parameters are interpreted.

**Compositional implication:** design tension trajectories by coordinating
several parameters, and avoid equating climax with loudness alone.

- Roni Y. Granot and Zohar Eitan, “Musical Tension and the Interaction of
  Dynamic Auditory Parameters”, *Music Perception* 28(3), 2011.
  DOI: 10.1525/MP.2011.28.3.219.

## Composition and performance contribute differently

Manipulations of pitch, mode, melodic progression, rhythm, tempo, sound level,
articulation and timbre predict different perceived emotions. Expressive timing
and intensity make an additional contribution, particularly to arousal.

**Compositional implication:** encode performance shape in the score and audio
render rather than relying on pitch and duration alone.

- Patrik N. Juslin and Erik Lindström, “Musical expression of emotions:
  modelling listeners’ judgements of composed and performed features”,
  *Music Analysis* 29(1–3), 2010/2011.
  DOI: 10.1111/j.1468-2249.2011.00323.x.

## Qualifications

- Listener expertise, familiarity, enculturation, attention and context alter
  aesthetic response.
- Statistical regularities explain tendencies, not individual preference.
- Synthetic rendering can expose structure but cannot substitute for a skilled
  human interpretation.
- The skill therefore uses these findings as revision prompts and requires
  explicit disclosure of assumptions and remaining limitations.
